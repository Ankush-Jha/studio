How to use Studio

Studio is our in-house creative assistant. You tell it what you need in one sentence, it brings back finished Instagram content for the client, on brand, ready for you to review. No design or coding needed.

I'm on

1What Studio does

It makes Instagram content for our clients:

Graphic reelsAnimated films in any of the styles in the Film Directory
Carousels & postsSwipe-through slides and single posts
Covers & storiesReel covers, story frames
Short loopsGIFs and quick motion hooks
Footage reelsCut from a client's own video, with captions

It already knows every client: their logo, colours, photos, licence details and how they like to sound. It checks its own facts and numbers, and it never posts anything. You stay in charge of what goes out.

2Get set up (once)

This takes about 30 minutes, most of it waiting. If someone on the team can do it for you, even easier.

  1. Install Claude Code. It's the app Studio runs in. Get it from claude.com/claude-code and sign in with your Claude account.
  2. Install Homebrew. It's a free helper that installs the tools Studio needs. Follow the one step on brew.sh.
  3. Install Studio. Open the Terminal app (press ⌘ Space, type Terminal, press Enter), paste this line and press Enter. You don't need to understand it:
    git clone https://github.com/Ankush-Jha/studio.git ~/studio && git clone https://github.com/Ankush-Jha/studio-clients.git ~/studio/private && cd ~/studio && brew bundle --file=Brewfile && sh setup.sh
    It downloads everything (about 3 GB) and finishes with a check. If every line ends in "ok", you're ready.
  1. Turn on Linux for Windows (WSL). It's a free part of Windows 10 and 11 that Studio runs inside. Click Start, type PowerShell, right-click it and choose Run as administrator. Paste this, press Enter, then restart your PC when it asks:
    wsl --install
    After the restart an Ubuntu window opens. Pick a username and a password, and remember the password: setup asks for it once.
  2. Install Studio. In that Ubuntu window, paste this line and press Enter. You don't need to understand it. It also installs Claude Code, the app Studio runs in:
    sudo apt-get update && sudo apt-get install -y git && git clone https://github.com/Ankush-Jha/studio.git ~/studio && git clone https://github.com/Ankush-Jha/studio-clients.git ~/studio/private && cd ~/studio && sh install-wsl.sh
    It downloads everything (about 3 GB) and finishes with a check. If every line ends in "ok", you're ready. The first time you open Studio, it asks you to sign in to your Claude account.
Planning to make reels from client video footage? After setup, open Studio and say "set up footage reels". That adds about 1 GB more. (On Windows, footage reels aren't supported yet. Everything else works.)

3Open Studio

Every time you want to work: open Terminal, paste this, press Enter.

Every time you want to work: open the Ubuntu app from the Start menu, paste this, press Enter.

cd ~/studio && claude

Using the Claude desktop app instead? Open Claude Code there and choose the studio folder in your home folder.

Tip: in Ubuntu, paste with right-click (or Ctrl+Shift+V).

Studio is now listening. From here on you just type normal sentences. A good first one:

get the latestStudio pulls in anything new your teammates added: new clients, lessons, finished work.

4Ask for a piece

Start with /create, then say who it's for, what it is and what it's about. That's all it needs. (Sam Rivera is our example client; use any real client's name.)

/create graphic reel for Sam Rivera about private mortgages /create carousel for Sam Rivera about renewing in 2027 /create static for Sam Rivera about first-time buyer myths /create story for Sam Rivera about the Bank of Canada rate decision /create footage reel for Sam Rivera from my Downloads folder, clip.mp4

Want more control? Add any of these

  • A style: "…in Art Deco style". Browse them all in the Film Directory, every style has a demo.
  • A length: "…40 seconds".
  • A mood or angle: "…keep it calm and reassuring", "…lead with the savings number".
  • Several clients at once: "/create carousel for group 2 on the rate decision". Each client gets their own, different version.

Not sure what to make? Just type "/create carousel for Sam Rivera" and Studio suggests topics from this week's news and what's working for similar accounts.

5The two check-ins

Studio stops twice to ask you. Nothing moves forward without your answer.

Check-in 1: pick an idea

You get three different ideas, A, B and C, each with its hook and look. Reply with:

Bor "B but with A's opening line", or "none of these, make it more playful"

Check-in 2: approve the finished piece

You see the finished reel or slides, the caption and Studio's own quality report. Reply with:

approveor your notes, in plain words: "make the logo bigger", "slide 3 has too much text"

Making a piece takes from about 10 minutes (posts and carousels) to an hour (animated reels). You can do other things meanwhile.

6Find your files

When it's done, Studio tells you where everything is: the video or the numbered slides, the cover, the caption ready to paste, and subtitles for reels.

where are my files?Studio opens the folder for you, in Finder on a Mac or File Explorer on Windows.

You upload to Instagram yourself. Studio reminds you of anything to tick, such as the AI label when a synthetic voice was used.

7The Desk, if you prefer clicking

The Desk is a visual control room in your web browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge…). It shows every piece in progress, lets you approve ideas and finished work with a click, previews reels in a phone frame, and lists every client.

/desk

8Teach it your taste

Studio learns from you, so it gets better every week:

  • Your notes at the check-ins are remembered for that client.
  • Your edits: if you fix a caption or a slide yourself, Studio notices the change.
  • Once in a while, say "/create learn". Studio suggests lessons from your notes and edits ("Sam prefers fewer words per slide"). Say which to keep, and it follows them from then on.

Every client can also have a standing brief, for example "always minimalist, sales-focused, private mortgages". Ask Studio to show or change a client's brief any time.

9End of the day

Before you close up, say:

save today's workStudio saves the finished pieces, notes and lessons to the shared space, so the whole team has them.

And at the start of the next session, "get the latest" brings in everyone else's work.

10What it will never do

  • Post or upload anything. Publishing is always you.
  • Make up facts. If a detail is missing (a licence number, a rate), it leaves a visible note instead of guessing.
  • Copy other accounts. It studies what works in the market through official sources, then makes its own.
  • Use an unconfirmed photo or logo. If an image is marked "check", you confirm it in the Desk first.
PROOF on a piece means a required detail is missing. In Ontario that's the brokerage name and licence number. The piece is fine to review and show the client, but don't post it until the detail is added.

11If something's off

check my setupStudio runs its health check and fixes what it can. /create statusShows every piece and where it's up to. continue the Sam Rivera reelPicks a stopped piece back up where it left off.

Or just describe the problem in your own words ("the reel has no sound", "it used the wrong logo"). Studio will look into it.

12Words you'll see

Style
The look of a piece. All film styles are in the Film Directory.
Check-in (gate)
A point where Studio waits for your decision: one for the idea, one for the finished piece.
Kit
Everything Studio knows about a client: brand, photos, licence, voice, past work.
Brief
Your one-sentence request, or a client's standing instructions.
PROOF
A piece that's missing a required detail and shouldn't be posted yet.
The Desk
The visual control room in your browser.
Radar
Studio's view of what's trending for similar accounts, used to suggest topics.
