# Lessons from competitor research (Nov 2026)

Weekly findings from public broker and real-estate channels (YouTube Shorts and long-form, Instagram) that are candidates for `PLAYBOOK.md`. Multiples are views against each channel's own median for that format. Promote a lesson into the playbook only after it holds for more than one week; review the list monthly, because what works shifts.

## Week of 16 Nov 2026
- **Lead with the person, then the lesson.** "Person in trouble" and surprise-bill stories beat advice even on the biggest channels (one at 1.8× the channel norm; a tax-authority renovation story at 2.7×). Make "story first" the default for at least one post per client group per week.
- **Put a face and a verdict on policy posts.** Faceless brand budget content got 119 views while a broker's on-camera verdict got 46K. Use a real photo of the broker on policy-post covers where the account allows it.
- **Distress stats are crowded.** Use power-of-sale and arrears numbers as context inside a renewal plan, never as the headline.
- **Check the next twist weekly.** Bond yields move fixed rates; "fixed may be turning back up" was an unclaimed angle that week.
- **Maths reminders:** a HELOC is interest-only, so ≈ $21 per $100K per 0.25%. Amortizing at the then-current variable rates (3.65% → 3.90%) ≈ $13.40 per $100K.

## 17 Nov 2026
- **A short sweeping claim viewers want to check.** A one-line sweeping claim title reached 1.9× a channel's long-form median while a vague strategy title sat at 0.5×. Hook pattern: one accurate, checkable claim tied to a rule that affects the viewer ("Budget 2026 didn't change your approval rules"), with proposals labelled as proposals.
- **Tell trap stories at full length, then turn them to "you".** A tax-trap story ran 2.1× at full length while the same topic as Shorts ran 1.1–1.4×. Client-story reels run 50–60 s, switch to second person after the payoff, and get no 15 s cut-down.
- **A policy number needs a consequence.** A permanent-resident admissions number with its effect on buyers hit 2.3×; a bare population number got 0.3×. Every statistic in a hook says what it means for the buyer or newcomer watching.
- **Distress stats stay context (second week running).** Power-of-sale Shorts ran 0.4–0.55× their norm. Rule: arrears and power-of-sale numbers never go in a hook.
- **Hold for unread releases and stale odds.** When a scheduled release (e.g. national sales data) hasn't been read, plan posts on confirmed local figures with no national stand-in. Quote market odds only with a date after the latest big data print (CPI, jobs); otherwise show both outcomes and leave the number out.
- **Headline-only figures stay off screen.** A figure read only from a headline went into a plan on screen. A figure marked "check" in research can go in a caption as attributed context at most, never on screen.

## 19 Nov 2026
- **Answer the either/or in the title.** "Stocks or buy a home? You can do both" reached 1.4× a channel's long-form median while its tax-rule Shorts ran 0.7–0.8×. Hook pattern: name the two choices the viewer is stuck between and give the answer (split mortgage, rent vs buy by city, wait vs move up), never just "it depends".
- **Name who the rule catches and what they must do.** A buyer-facing withholding-tax rule Short ran 1.6× while general renovation Shorts on the same channel ran 0.5–0.8×. Every rule or policy post says in the hook who it applies to, and in the payoff the one action to take.
- **Judge warning-style posts and trial-reel hooks after 48 hours, not on day one.** A fraud-warning post went from 0.7× on day one to 1.6× two days later. When comparing two trial-reel hooks, wait 48 hours before keeping one.
- **Every number in a hook carries its consequence (third week running).** Arrears and unpaid-tax Shorts paired with what happens next ran 1.4–1.5×, while a plain sales recap ran 0.7× twice. Extend the rule from policy figures to market and rate statistics.
- **Named people in story posts are labelled composites.** Named-person titles keep winning (one at 2.4×). Unless a real client has signed off, the person is a composite and the piece says "Illustrative example" on screen, so a story never reads as a real testimonial or record.
